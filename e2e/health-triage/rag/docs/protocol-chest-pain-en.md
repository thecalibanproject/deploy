# Chest pain triage protocol

Cedar Valley Medical Center, emergency department, Beirut. Protocol ED-07, revision 4.
A fictional protocol written for the Caliban end-to-end test; it is not medical guidance.

## Scope

This protocol applies to every adult who arrives at the emergency department with chest pain,
chest tightness or pressure, pain that spreads to the arm, jaw or back, or palpitations with
dizziness. Children are assessed under the pediatric protocol.

## On arrival

1. The triage nurse records an ECG within 10 minutes of arrival, before registration is complete.
2. Vital signs are taken at the same time: heart rate, blood pressure on both arms, oxygen
   saturation and temperature.
3. The ECG is shown to the emergency physician on duty within 5 more minutes.

## Triage categories

| Category | Signs | Seen by a physician |
|---|---|---|
| Red | ST elevation on the ECG, low blood pressure, fainting | At once |
| Orange | Ongoing pain with sweating or breathlessness | Within 15 minutes |
| Yellow | Pain that has stopped, normal ECG, normal vital signs | Within 60 minutes |
| Green | Pain clearly linked to movement or touch, normal ECG | Within 2 hours |

## Escalation

A red category patient goes straight to the resuscitation bay and the cardiology team on call
is paged. The catheterisation laboratory at Cedar Valley is open around the clock. For an orange
category patient, a troponin blood test is taken on arrival and repeated after 3 hours.

## Discharge

A patient may go home only after two normal troponin results, a normal ECG and a review by the
emergency physician. The patient leaves with a written summary and a cardiology consultation
within 7 days.
